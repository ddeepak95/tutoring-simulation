Use this rubric to evaluate the educational quality of chatbot responses to a high school learner. Score each dimension **3 (Effective)**, **2 (Partially effective)**, or **1 (Ineffective)**. Use **N/A** only when a behavior is not reasonably called for in the evaluated response.

# Initial Teaching Response

### Topic Introduction and Learner Engagement

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot introduce the topic, spark the learner's interest, and explore their prior knowledge and learning goals?

- **3 — Effective:** Introduces the topic in a way that sparks curiosity or excitement. Makes a meaningful attempt to learn what the student already knows and what they want to learn before proceeding.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Introduces the topic but makes limited effort to engage the student or explore prior knowledge and goals. Questions may be generic or superficial.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Jumps directly into explaining the topic without attempting to engage the student, assess prior knowledge, or understand learning goals.

### Amount of Information

**Question:** How appropriately does the chatbot adjust the amount and depth of information to a high school learner's needs?

- **3 — Effective:** Provides enough detail to make the topic understandable, focuses on essential ideas, and presents information in manageable amounts without unnecessary repetition.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Provides a generally useful explanation but is somewhat too brief, overly detailed, repetitive, or unevenly paced.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Gives too little information to understand the topic, or overwhelms the learner with excessive, irrelevant, or poorly organized detail.

### Naturalness of the Conversation

**Question:** How natural, approachable, and conversational is the chatbot's response?

- **3 — Effective:** Uses clear, friendly, age-appropriate language and a smooth conversational flow that makes the learner feel comfortable participating.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Is understandable but at times feels scripted, overly formal, repetitive, or awkward.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Feels robotic, disjointed, or unnatural in a way that makes the exchange difficult to follow or discourages interaction.

### Examples, Analogies, and Real-World Connections

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot use examples, analogies, or real-world connections to explain the concept?

- **3 — Effective:** Uses accurate, relevant, and accessible examples, analogies, or real-world connections that clearly help the learner understand the concept.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Includes an example, analogy, or connection, but it is generic, underdeveloped, or only loosely linked to the concept.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Uses confusing or misleading examples, or omits helpful examples and connections so that the concept remains difficult to understand.

### Clarifying Misconceptions

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot anticipate or clarify common misconceptions a learner might have about the topic?

- **3 — Effective:** Identifies a relevant likely misconception or point of confusion and explains the correct idea clearly, including why the mistaken interpretation is inaccurate.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Mentions a possible misconception or source of confusion but only partly resolves it, leaving the distinction unclear.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Ignores an important likely misconception, reinforces an incorrect belief, or introduces further confusion.

### Memory Aids and Key Takeaways

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot use memory aids such as mnemonics, key takeaways, or summaries to help the learner remember the concept?

- **3 — Effective:** Reinforces the most important ideas with a concise, accurate, and useful summary, key takeaway, mnemonic, or other memory aid.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Attempts reinforcement, but the memory aid or summary is vague, overly long, incomplete, or not clearly connected to the central idea.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Provides no useful reinforcement when it would help, or uses a misleading or confusing memory aid.

### Checking Understanding

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot check the learner's understanding of the concept?

- **3 — Effective:** Gives the learner a specific, relevant opportunity to explain, predict, apply, or reason about the concept.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Offers only a generic check such as “Does that make sense?”, a simple recall question, or an unfocused practice prompt.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Does not attempt a meaningful understanding check, or asks a question unrelated to what the learner should understand.

### Guidance for Next Steps

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot guide the learner toward an appropriate next step in learning?

- **3 — Effective:** Suggests a clear, relevant next action, such as practice, application, reflection, or a related topic, that builds on the learner's current understanding.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Suggests a next step, but it is vague, generic, or only loosely connected to the topic.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Gives no useful direction for continuing, or suggests an irrelevant or confusing next step.

# Subsequent Conversation Turns

Replace Topic Introduction and Learner Engagement with Addressing Follow-Up Questions. Retain the other seven criteria, giving eight criteria per tutor turn.

### Addressing Follow-Up Questions

**Question:** How effectively does the chatbot respond to the learner's follow-up question while using the context of the earlier conversation?

- **3 — Effective:** Directly and accurately answers the follow-up, builds on what has already been discussed, and adjusts the explanation to the learner's specific question or confusion.

- **2 — Partially effective:** Addresses the follow-up only partly, misses some of the learner's concern, or repeats earlier information without sufficient adaptation.

- **1 — Ineffective:** Fails to answer the follow-up, ignores relevant prior context, or gives an irrelevant, confusing, or incorrect response.

# Scoring Guidance

Use **3 (Effective)**, **2 (Partially effective)**, or **1 (Ineffective)** for each question. For criteria involving optional teaching techniques, use **N/A** only if the technique is genuinely unnecessary in the response being evaluated. An omitted technique that would have helped the learner should receive a low score.
