# Sources — `tree-mass`

The literature backing for each entry in [tree-mass.json](tree-mass.json). Kept out of the JSON
because no code reads it; kept in the repo because the grounding claim is only as good as this list.

Every URL below was fetched and confirmed to state the conception by an agent that did not do the
original research (Stage 2 of [misconception_library.md](../../docs/misconception_library.md)),
2026-07-28.

## `tree-mass.from-the-soil` — A tree's mass comes from the soil

- **AAAS Project 2061 Science Assessment, MEM123**, "Substances in soil are food for plants"
  (Kuech et al. 2003; Leach et al. 1992; Simpson & Arnold 1982; Stavy et al. 1987; Tamir 1989;
  Wandersee 1983). Item ME109003: **72% of 2,961 US grade 6–8 students** chose an answer keeping
  soil as a food source. <https://assess.bscs.org/science/misconceptions/1/ME/80/MEM123>
- **AAAS Project 2061, MEM013**, "Food enters a plant through the roots".
  <https://assess.bscs.org/science/misconceptions/1/ME/80/MEM013>
- **Skribe Dimec, D., & Strgar, J. (2017).** Scientific conceptions of photosynthesis among primary
  school pupils and student teachers of biology. *CEPS Journal*, 7(1), 49–68. Restates, crediting
  the Leeds National Curriculum Science Support Project (Driver et al., 1992), that the universal
  and very persistent intuitive conception across all ages is that plants get their food from their
  environment, especially the soil. *Secondary report, not the originating study.*
  <https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1137843.pdf>
- **Dauer, J. M., Doherty, J. H., Freed, A. L., & Anderson, C. W. (2014).** Connections between
  student explanations and arguments from evidence about plant growth. *CBE—Life Sciences
  Education*, 13(3), 397–409. Attributes to Driver et al. (1994) and others that students indicate
  soil or water as the source of matter for plant growth, and reports that most students do not read
  a van Helmont-like experiment as discrepant with their own beliefs.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152202/>

## `tree-mass.from-the-water` — A tree's mass comes from the water it was given

- **AAAS Project 2061, MEM082**, "Plants make sugars from minerals (Tamir, 1989) or minerals and
  water (AAAS pilot data 2006)". Item ME095006: **25% of grade 6–8 and 33% of grade 9–12** students
  chose that sugar molecules are made from water and minerals.
  <https://assess.bscs.org/science/misconceptions/1/ME/80/MEM082>
- **Dauer et al. (2014)**, as above. Notes that van Helmont-style evidence does not account for
  other possible sources of mass such as water, and that even their strongest student did not
  observe that the evidence failed to eliminate water.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152202/>

## `tree-mass.growing-is-just-what-trees-do` — Growth is something the tree does, not stuff moving

- **Dauer et al. (2014)**, as above. Reports students reasoning about plant growth as an action
  enabled by water, air, sunlight and soil rather than as a transformation of matter, distinguishing
  "actors" from "enablers"; 3 of 22 students interviewed after instruction gave such explanations,
  and none of them read the mass experiments as being about tracing material.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152202/>
