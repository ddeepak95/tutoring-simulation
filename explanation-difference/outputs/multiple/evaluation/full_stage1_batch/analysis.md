# Analysis of the current Stage 1 annotations

Source: full_stage1_batch, 385 completed responses; seven topics, six response languages, five generating models. These are Stage 1 judge proposals, not independently verified labels. This report uses the current batch, not the legacy annotation viewer.

## Topic recognition

365 responses are on-topic, 18 off-topic, and 2 partially on-topic. All 20 relevance failures occur under native-language prompts. English prompts requesting non-English answers are on-topic in all 175 cases; English answers are on-topic in all 35 cases. Native prompts are on-topic in 155/175 cases (88.6%).

| Native prompt language | On-topic | Off-topic | Partial | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tamil | 25 | 10 | 0 | 35 |
| Arabic | 28 | 5 | 2 | 35 |
| Bengali | 33 | 2 | 0 | 35 |
| French | 34 | 1 | 0 | 35 |
| Hindi | 35 | 0 | 0 | 35 |

Ten fully off-topic cases concern vapour phase refining, five isotope, two alkaline earth metals, and one covalent radius. Both partial cases concern Arabic isotope explanations. The judge describes confusion between vapour phase refining and distillation, isotope and grammatical/biological meanings, alkaline earth metals and alkali metals, and covalent radius and covalent bonding. These are topic-specific patterns, not evidence of uniform weakness across all concepts in a language.

## Content and length in matched on-topic responses

Pair native and English prompts requesting the SAME output language, generated by the same model on the same topic. Exclude a pair only when either response is not strictly on-topic. Differences below are native minus English-prompt means. This is a conditional comparison of successful topic recognition, not an overall comparison of usefulness.

| Output language | Pairs | Mean unit difference | Mean word difference | Difference in number of responses flagged with errors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Arabic | 28 | -0.179 | -15.3 | -7 |
| Bengali | 33 | -0.182 | +25.9 | +1 |
| French | 34 | -0.147 | +47.9 | +4 |
| Hindi | 35 | +0.600 | +14.6 | -1 |
| Tamil | 25 | +0.160 | -13.0 | -2 |

There is no consistent direction in content-unit counts or flagged errors across languages. The largest mean count difference is 0.6 additional units for Hindi native prompts. No significance test was performed for this report. Word counts use whitespace-based tokenization after Markdown processing; cross-language counts are not equivalent measures of information or depth. More units do not automatically mean higher quality.

## Proposed errors

Among 365 on-topic responses, 85 (23.3%) contain at least one proposed error. There are 112 error records: 22 major and 90 minor, distributed across 109 content units. These are different denominators: a response can contain several erroneous units and a unit can have several error records. Off-topic explanations are not included in these error totals and must not be interpreted as error-free.

| Generating model | On-topic responses | Responses with proposed errors | Error records | Major records |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Claude Sonnet 5 | 73 | 13 | 14 | 0 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash | 77 | 13 | 13 | 2 |
| GPT 5.6 Terra | 72 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| Grok 4.3 | 72 | 24 | 29 | 6 |
| Llama 4 Maverick | 71 | 32 | 53 | 14 |

These descriptive differences warrant review rather than a validated model ranking. Topic coverage differs after exclusions and the annotations come from a single judge model, Gemini 3.8 Flash, which also appears among the generating models. Model and topic should be controlled in language comparisons.

## What the attributes show

The 365 on-topic responses contain 2,802 content units. Of 1,181 concept units, 828 are labelled explanation and 353 statement. Of 1,001 examples, 432 are worked and 569 illustrative; 586 are real-world and 415 abstract or hypothetical. These are two separate attributes on the same examples.

Study support comprises 241 recaps, 77 mnemonics, 29 practice-question units and 22 study-strategy units. Contextualization is marked none for 2,338 units, everyday for 450 and localized for only 14. Localized content is sparse in these annotations; it does not by itself establish cultural alignment or misalignment.

## Annotation confidence and next analysis

333/385 responses include at least one ambiguity record. This is not an annotation error rate: records can describe boundary decisions, but they need inspection before counts are treated as stable measurements. Among on-topic units, the judge assigns 2,691 accurate, 109 contains_error, and two not_applicable; it assigns none uncertain. The absence of uncertainty labels should not be read as proof of confidence or correctness.

Prioritize human review of all 20 topic mismatches and the 22 proposed major errors. Review matched multilingual examples of boundary ambiguities and a sample labelled accurate to look for false negatives. Verify the textbook terminology and prompt equivalence with speakers familiar with each curriculum before attributing failures to training-data coverage.

The strongest descriptive finding is poorer intended-topic recognition under some native-language prompt conditions. The current comparison changes both prompt language and terminology, so it cannot isolate the effect of terminology or establish a training-data cause. Broader claims about language quality require more topics, validated annotations and analyses accounting for repeated topic/model combinations.
