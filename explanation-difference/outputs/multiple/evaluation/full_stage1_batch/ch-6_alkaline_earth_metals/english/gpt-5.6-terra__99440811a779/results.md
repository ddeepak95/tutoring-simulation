```text
All alkaline earth metals have **two electrons in their outer shell**.

For example:

- Magnesium: 2,8,2  
- Calcium: 2,8,8,2  

Because they have two outer electrons, they usually lose both when reacting. They form ions with a **2+ charge**.

    "reason": "The trend of increasing reactivity down Group 2 and the underlying periodic trend explanation (increasing atomic radius, weaker electrostatic attraction, lower first and second ionization energies) are scientifically correct.",
    "errors": []
  }
}
```

```text
Reactivity generally **increases down Group 2**:

\[
\text{Be} < \text{Mg} < \text{Ca} < \text{Sr} < \text{Ba}
\]

This happens because, down the group:

1. The atoms become larger.
2. The outer electrons are further from the nucleus.
3. The attraction between the nucleus and the outer electrons becomes weaker.
4. The atoms lose their two outer electrons more easily.

Therefore, barium is more reactive than magnesium.
```

```text
### 2. Reaction with Water

The reactivity with water increases down the group.

- **Beryllium:** does not react easily with water.
- **Magnesium:** reacts very slowly with cold water, but reacts faster with steam.
- **Calcium:** reacts noticeably with cold water.
- **Strontium and barium:** react vigorously with water.

Example with calcium:

\[
\text{Ca} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{H}_2
\]

This produces:

- Calcium hydroxide, an alkaline solution
- Hydrogen gas
```
