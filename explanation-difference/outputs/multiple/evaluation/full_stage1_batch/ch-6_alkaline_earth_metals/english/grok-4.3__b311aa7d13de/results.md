### Why Are They Called “Alkaline Earth” Metals?
- Their **oxides** (compounds with oxygen) are **alkaline** — they make water basic (like soap or antacids).
- Long ago, scientists called these oxides “earths” because they didn’t dissolve easily in water and looked like soil or rock.
- So the name stuck: “alkaline earth metals.”

```text
### Key Properties (What They’re Like)
Alkaline earth metals share these traits:

1. **Two valence electrons**  
   Each atom has two electrons in its outer shell. They tend to lose both electrons to form +2 ions (for example, Mg²⁺ or Ca²⁺). This is why they’re less reactive than alkali metals, which only have to lose one electron.

2. **Shiny, silvery-white metals**  
   When freshly cut, they look bright and metallic. They conduct heat and electricity well.

3. **Reactivity with water and air**  
   - They react with water to produce hydrogen gas and a metal hydroxide, but much more slowly than sodium or potassium.  
   - Calcium reacts noticeably with water; magnesium needs hot water or steam; beryllium hardly reacts at all.

4. **They form basic oxides and hydroxides**  
   Their oxides and hydroxides turn red litmus paper blue — a classic sign they are bases.

5. **Trends as you go down the group**  
   - Atoms get bigger.  
   - It becomes easier to lose the two outer electrons.  
   - So reactivity **increases** from beryllium (least reactive) to radium (most reactive).
```
