**Vapour phase refining** is a method used to purify metals by converting the impure metal into a **volatile compound** (a compound that easily changes into vapour). This vapour is then decomposed to obtain the **pure metal**.

### Principle
Some metals form gaseous compounds with certain chemicals at low temperatures. When these gaseous compounds are heated at a higher temperature, they break down and deposit the pure metal.

### Steps involved
1. **Formation of a volatile compound**  
   The impure metal reacts with a suitable gas or chemical to form a volatile compound.

2. **Separation from impurities**  
   The volatile compound turns into vapour and separates from the non-volatile impurities, which are left behind.

3. **Decomposition of vapour**  
   The vapour is heated or passed over a hot surface. It decomposes and deposits pure metal.

---

## Example 1: Mond Process for Nickel

Nickel is purified using **carbon monoxide**.

### Step 1: Formation of nickel carbonyl
Impure nickel reacts with carbon monoxide at about **330–350 K**:

\[
\text{Ni} + 4\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{Ni(CO)}_4
\]

Nickel tetracarbonyl, \(\text{Ni(CO)}_4\), is a volatile gas.

### Step 2: Decomposition
The gas is heated to about **450–470 K**:

\[
\text{Ni(CO)}_4 \rightarrow \text{Ni} + 4\text{CO}
\]

Pure nickel is deposited, while carbon monoxide can be reused.

---

## Example 2: Van Arkel Process for Titanium and Zirconium

Titanium or zirconium is purified by reacting it with **iodine**.

For titanium:

\[
\text{Ti} + 2\text{I}_2 \rightarrow \text{TiI}_4
\]

Titanium tetraiodide (\(\text{TiI}_4\)) is volatile. Its vapour is passed over a hot tungsten filament, where it decomposes:

\[
\text{TiI}_4 \rightarrow \text{Ti} + 2\text{I}_2
\]

Pure titanium is deposited on the filament.

---

### In short
Vapour phase refining works because:

> **Impure metal → volatile compound → vapour → heating → pure metal**

It is mainly used for metals that can form volatile compounds, such as **nickel, titanium, and zirconium**.

Vapour phase refining is a method used to purify certain metals that can easily form volatile (easily vaporized) compounds. The idea is simple: turn the impure metal into a gas or vapour, separate it from the solid impurities that stay behind, and then break the vapour back down into pure metal.

Here's how it works in clear steps:

1. **Form the volatile compound**  
   The impure metal is heated with a suitable gas or element (like carbon monoxide or iodine). Only the desired metal reacts to form a compound that turns into vapour at a relatively low temperature. Most impurities do not form such vapours and remain as solid residue.

2. **Vaporisation and separation**  
   The volatile compound is heated gently so it becomes a gas. This gas is moved away (often through a pipe or chamber), leaving the non-volatile impurities behind. Because the compound is now in vapour form, it can be purified very effectively.

3. **Decomposition to recover pure metal**  
   The purified vapour is then heated to a higher temperature or passed over a hot surface (like a heated filament). This causes the compound to break down, depositing the pure metal as a solid while releasing the original gas or element, which can often be reused.

Two common examples taught in high school are:

- **Mond’s process for nickel**  
  Impure nickel is heated with carbon monoxide gas at about 50 °C to form nickel carbonyl vapour:  
  Ni (impure) + 4CO → Ni(CO)₄ (vapour)  
  The vapour is then heated to about 200 °C, where it decomposes:  
  Ni(CO)₄ → Ni (pure) + 4CO  
  The pure nickel deposits as a solid, and carbon monoxide is recycled.

- **Van Arkel process for titanium or zirconium**  
  The impure metal is heated with iodine to form a volatile iodide vapour. This vapour is passed over a hot tungsten filament (around 1400–1800 °C). The iodide breaks down, leaving a rod of very pure metal on the filament while iodine vapour is released and can be reused.

This method is especially useful when the metal forms a compound that is stable enough to vaporise but can be easily decomposed at higher temperatures. It gives metals of very high purity (often 99.9 % or more) and is used mainly for nickel, titanium, zirconium, and a few other reactive metals.

### What is Vapour Phase Refining?
In simple terms: **It is a method where an impure metal is converted into a gas (vapour), separated from its impurities, and then broken down again to get the pure metal.**

This is used when we need metals with **ultra-high purity** (for things like space technology or electronics).

### The Two Golden Rules
To use this method, the metal must follow two strict rules:
1. **Easy to turn into gas:** The metal must easily react with a chemical at a relatively low temperature to form a **volatile compound** (a compound that turns into a gas easily).
2. **Easy to break down:** That gas must easily decompose (break apart) at a higher temperature to give back the pure metal.

*(Crucially: The impurities mixed with the metal must **not** react, so they stay behind as solids).*

---

### The 2-Step Process

#### **Step 1: Formation of the Volatile Compound**
We take the impure metal and react it with a specific chemical reagent at a lower temperature. The metal combines with this chemical and turns into a gas. The impurities don't react and are left behind.
$$\text{Impure Metal (Solid)} + \text{Reagent} \xrightarrow{\text{Lower Temp}} \text{Metal-Compound (Gas)}$$

#### **Step 2: Decomposition (Breaking it down)**
We collect that gas, move it to another chamber, and heat it to a much higher temperature. The compound breaks apart: the pure metal deposits as a solid, and the reagent gas flies away (and can be reused!).
$$\text{Metal-Compound (Gas)} \xrightarrow{\text{Higher Temp}} \text{Pure Metal (Solid)} + \text{Reagent}$$

*   **Step 2:** This gas is then heated to a higher temperature ($200^\circ\text{C}$ or $\approx 450 - 470\text{ K}$). It breaks apart, giving pure solid Nickel and releasing the $\text{CO}$ gas.
    $$\text{Ni(CO)}_4 \rightarrow \text{Ni (Pure Solid)} + 4\text{CO}$$

#### 2. Van Arkel-de Boer Method (Used for **Zirconium** or **Titanium**)

# Vapour Phase Refining

## What is it?

**Vapour Phase Refining** is a method used to purify metals by converting them into a **volatile compound**, which is then decomposed to give back the **pure metal**. This technique is used when a metal has impurities that are difficult to remove by other methods like electrolytic refining.

## Basic Principle

The method works on a simple two-step idea:

1. **Step 1:** The impure metal is converted into a **volatile compound** (a compound that easily vaporizes) by reacting it with a suitable substance.
2. **Step 2:** This volatile compound is then **decomposed** (usually by heating) to give the **pure metal**.

### Key Requirements:
- The volatile compound must be **easy to form** from the impure metal.
- The compound must be **easily decomposable** to give the pure metal back.

---

## Important Examples

### 1. **Mond's Process (for Nickel)**

Used to refine impure nickel.

**Step 1:** Impure nickel is heated in a stream of **carbon monoxide (CO)** gas at about 330–350 K. This forms a volatile compound called **nickel tetracarbonyl**.

$$Ni + 4CO \xrightarrow{330-350K} Ni(CO)_4$$

**Step 2:** The nickel tetracarbonyl vapor is then heated to a higher temperature (450–470 K), where it **decomposes** to give pure nickel and releases CO gas (which can be reused).

$$Ni(CO)_4 \xrightarrow{450-470K} Ni \text{ (pure)} + 4CO$$

---

### 2. **Van Arkel Method (for Zirconium or Titanium)**

Used for removing **oxygen and nitrogen impurities** from metals like zirconium and titanium.

**Step 1:** Impure metal is heated with **iodine** to form a volatile compound.

$$Zr + 2I_2 \rightarrow ZrI_4$$

**Step 2:** The zirconium iodide vapor is then decomposed on a **very hot tungsten filament** (about 1800 K), giving pure zirconium.

$$ZrI_4 \xrightarrow{1800K} Zr \text{ (pure)} + 2I_2$$

The iodine gas released can be reused to purify more metal.

---

## Simple Diagram of the Process

```
Impure Metal  +  Suitable Reagent  →  Volatile Compound
                                              ↓
                                         (Heating/Decomposition)
                                              ↓
                                        Pure Metal + Reagent (reused)
```

---

## Why is this method useful?

- It gives **very high purity** metals.
- The **reagent (CO or Iodine) is recycled**, making the process efficient.
- It's especially useful for metals that are hard to purify by other chemical methods.

---

## Quick Memory Trick 🧠

Think of it like **"packing and unpacking"**:
- You "pack" the impure metal into a **volatile (gaseous) box** (the compound).
- Then you "unpack" it by heating, and only the **pure metal comes out**, leaving impurities behind!

**Purification through vapor state** (Distillation) is a method of separating liquids in a mixture and obtaining a pure liquid. This is based on the **difference in the boiling points** of liquids.

### Basic concept
When a liquid is heated, it turns into vapor. If that vapor is cooled, it turns back into a liquid. The liquid obtained in this manner is, in many instances, of higher purity than the initial mixture.

### Example: Obtaining pure water from salt water
Water and salt are mixed in salt water.

1. We heat the salt water in a vessel.
2. Water boils at about **100°C** and turns into vapor.
3. Salt does not vaporize; it will remain behind in the vessel itself.
4. We pass the formed water vapor through a cold tube.
5. The water vapor cools and turns back into water.
6. The water collected in this manner is called **boiled and filtered water** or **distilled water**.

### Important parts
- **Boiling vessel** – The place where the mixture is heated  
- **Vapor tube** – The path that carries the formed vapor  
- **Cooler (condenser)** – The device that cools the vapor and converts it into liquid  
- **Collection vessel** – The place where the pure liquid collects  

### When is it used?
- To produce drinking water from seawater
- To obtain pure water in laboratories
- To separate petroleum into multiple parts
- To manufacture perfumes and certain medicinal products

### Points to remember
Purification through vapor state is a **method of obtaining the vaporizing substance separately**. Non-vaporizing impurities, salt, sand, etc., will be left behind.

**In brief:**  
Heating → Vaporization → Cooling → Obtaining pure liquid.

# Vapor Phase Purification (Vapor Phase Refining/Distillation)

## Basic Concept

Vapor phase purification is a method of separating **mixtures using their boiling point difference**. It is used to **purify solids and separate liquid mixtures**.

## Scientific Basis

- Every substance has a **unique boiling point (Boiling Point)**
- When raising the temperature, the substance with the lower boiling point first **vaporizes** and separates
- When cooling this vapor and **liquefying it again (Condensation)**, a pure substance is obtained

## Main Types

### 1. **Filtration Vaporization (Sublimation)**
- The method in which a solid directly vaporizes and becomes solid again
- Example: Used to purify **iodine (Iodine), camphor (Camphor), naphthalene**

### 2. **Fractional Distillation (Fractional Distillation)**
- The method of separating individually from a mixture of many substances based on the difference in boiling points
- Example: Separating things like **petrol, kerosene, diesel from crude oil (Crude Oil)**

## Steps

1. **Heating** - Heating the mixture
2. **Vaporization** - Vaporization of the substance with the lower boiling point
3. **Cooling** - Collecting the vapor on a cold surface
4. **Re-liquefaction (Condensation)** - The vapor turning back into liquid

## Applications in Daily Life

| Application | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Drinking water purification | Removal of salt from seawater |
| Liquor preparation | Extraction of alcohol |
| Oil refining | Production of petroleum products |
| Metallurgy | Purifying certain metals (such as zinc) |

## Importance

Because this method is used **to obtain uncontaminated, pure substances**, it plays a very important role in the pharmaceutical industry, chemical research, and industries.

**In brief:** Vapor phase purification is a simple yet powerful scientific method of separating mixtures by "heating-vaporizing-cooling".

Hello! In Chemistry, in the section on the extraction of metals, a very important and interesting topic is **"Vapour Phase Refining"**. 

Let us look at this very simply, just like a teacher explains it to you in a classroom.

---

### What is Vapour Phase Refining? (Basic Concept)
The meaning is right in the name—**"Vapour"**. 
An impure metal should be heated along with a suitable chemical substance to convert it into an **easily vaporizable compound (Volatile Compound)**. Then, if that vapour is subjected to an even higher temperature and decomposed (decompose), we will get a **100% pure metal**.

To put it briefly:
> **Impure metal + chemical substance $\rightarrow$ volatile gas (impurities will remain behind) $\rightarrow$ heating at high heat $\rightarrow$ pure metal.**

---

### For this, 2 important conditions (Two Golden Rules) are needed:
1. The metal we choose should react with a specific reagent (reagent) to **change into an easily vaporizable compound (volatile compound).**
2. When that vapour compound is heated, it should **easily break (decompose) and give back the pure metal.**

---

For your school examinations, the **two important examples** coming under this method are very, very important:

#### 1. Mond Process – Refining of Nickel:
The metal nickel can be refined very easily by this method. This happens in two steps:

* **Step 1:** When impure nickel is added with **carbon monoxide ($CO$)** gas and heated at a temperature of about $350 \text{ K}$, a gas called **"nickel tetracarbonyl"** $[Ni(CO)_4]$ is formed. The impurities present in nickel will remain as they are without vaporizing.
  $$\text{Ni (impure)} + 4\text{CO} \xrightarrow{350\text{ K}} \text{Ni(CO)}_4 \text{ (vapour)}$$

* **Step 2:** Now this gas alone should be separated out and heated to a temperature of about $450 - 470 \text{ K}$. Then that compound breaks, and **pure nickel** separately and carbon monoxide gas separately will split apart.
  $$\text{Ni(CO)}_4 \xrightarrow{450 - 470\text{ K}} \text{Ni (pure metal)} + 4\text{CO}$$

---

#### 2. Van Arkel Method – Titanium (Ti) / Zirconium (Zr):
This method is used to remove impurities like oxygen, nitrogen in metals like titanium and zirconium.

* **Step 1:** Impure titanium should be combined with **iodine ($I_2$)** and heated in an evacuated vessel at a temperature of about $550 \text{ K}$. Then a vapour called **"titanium tetraiodide"** ($TiI_4$) will form.
  $$\text{Ti (impure)} + 2\text{I}_2 \xrightarrow{550\text{ K}} \text{TiI}_4 \text{ (vapour)}$$

* **Step 2:** They will pass this vapour over a **Tungsten filament** glowing at a very high heat (about $1800 \text{ K}$). Due to high heat it decomposes, and **pure titanium** will deposit on that tungsten filament. Iodine will escape as gas (this can be reused).
  $$\text{TiI}_4 \xrightarrow{1800\text{ K}} \text{Ti (pure metal)} + 2\text{I}_2$$

---

### 'Cheat Code' to remember:
* **Mond Process** $\rightarrow$ Nickel ($Ni$) $\rightarrow$ Carbon monoxide ($CO$)
* **Van Arkel Process** $\rightarrow$ Titanium ($Ti$) / Zirconium ($Zr$) $\rightarrow$ Iodine ($I_2$)

# Vapour Phase Refining

## Introduction

Vapour phase refining is one of the many methods of purifying (refining) metals. In this method, **the impure metal is combined with an inorganic gas (compound), converted into an easily volatile compound**, and then by decomposing that vapour, the pure metal is obtained.

## Principle

This method takes place in two steps:

1. **Step 1:** Impure metal + a gas (reagent) → **Volatile compound**
   
2. **Step 2:** Extracting the pure metal by **decomposing (decomposition)** this volatile compound.

**Important condition:** 
- The compound formed **must vaporize easily**
- It **must be easily decomposable**
- Impurities must not react with this compound

## Examples

### 1. Mond's Process - Nickel refining

**Step 1:** When impure nickel is heated with carbon monoxide (CO) gas at a temperature of 330-350 K, volatile **nickel tetracarbonyl** is formed.

$$Ni + 4CO \xrightarrow{330-350K} Ni(CO)_4$$

**Step 2:** If this vapour is brought to a high temperature of 450-470 K, it decomposes to give pure nickel.

$$Ni(CO)_4 \xrightarrow{450-470K} Ni + 4CO$$

Here, CO gas is used repeatedly.

### 2. Van Arkel Method - Zr/Ti refining

This is used to remove impurities like **nitrogen and oxygen** from metals like **zirconium (Zr)** and **titanium (Ti)**.

**Step 1:** When the impure metal is heated with iodine (I₂), a volatile compound is formed.

$$Zr + 2I_2 \xrightarrow{870K} ZrI_4$$

**Step 2:** When this vapour is passed over a tungsten filament having a very high temperature (1800 K), it decomposes and pure zirconium deposits over the filament.

$$ZrI_4 \xrightarrow{1800K} Zr + 2I_2$$

## Summary

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Method | Converting impure metal into a volatile compound and then decomposing |
| Example 1 | Ni + CO → Ni(CO)₄ → Ni (Mond's process) |
| Example 2 | Zr + I₂ → ZrI₄ → Zr (Van Arkel method) |
| Use | To obtain metals with high purity |

Through this method, metals with **99.9% purity** can be obtained, which is very important in fields such as electronics and space technology.
