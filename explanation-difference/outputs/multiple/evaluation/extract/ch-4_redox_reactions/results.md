### Subtopics

- **s1: Redox reactions definition** (explained)

> Redox reactions (short for **reduction-oxidation**) are chemical reactions where electrons move from one substance to another.

- **s2: Oxidation** (explained)

> **Oxidation** = loss of electrons (think “OIL” – Oxidation Is Loss).  
>   The atom or ion that loses electrons becomes more positive.

- **s3: Reduction** (explained)

> **Reduction** = gain of electrons (think “RIG” – Reduction Is Gain).  
>   The atom or ion that gains electrons becomes more negative.

- **s4: Coupling of oxidation and reduction** (explained)

> These two processes are always paired—like two sides of the same coin. You can’t have one without the other.

- **s5: Oxidation numbers** (explained)

> Chemists use **oxidation numbers** (or oxidation states) to track electron movement:
> 
> 1. An element that starts at 0 and ends with a positive number has been **oxidized** (lost electrons).  
> 2. An element that starts at 0 and ends with a negative number has been **reduced** (gained electrons).

- **s6: Oxidizing agent** (explained)

> The substance that causes oxidation (by taking electrons) is the **oxidizing agent**.

- **s7: Reducing agent** (explained)

> The substance that causes reduction (by giving electrons) is the **reducing agent**.

- **s8: OIL RIG mnemonic** (explained)

> **OIL RIG** → Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

### Examples

- **e1: Rusting of iron** (illustrative_example)

> Simplified equation:  
> **4 Fe + 3 O₂ → 2 Fe₂O₃**
> 
> - Iron atoms lose electrons (they are oxidized).  
> - Oxygen atoms gain those electrons (they are reduced).

- **e2: Zinc and copper sulfate displacement reaction** (illustrative_example)

> **Zn (zinc) + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (copper)**
> 
> Here, zinc metal gives away electrons to copper ions in solution. Zinc is oxidized; copper ions are reduced and turn into shiny copper metal that coats the zinc.

> **combustion** (burning) are also redox reactions.

### Subtopics

- **s1: Redox reactions definition** (explained)

> Reactions in which one element undergoing oxidation and another element undergoing reduction occur at the same time in a chemical reaction are called redox reactions.

- **s2: Oxidation and reduction via electron transfer** (explained)

> the reaction in which an electron is given is called an oxidation reaction, and the reaction in which an electron is received is called a reduction reaction.

- **s3: Oxidizing and reducing agents** (explained)

> the substance that gives away electrons acts as a reducing agent (reducing agent), and the substance that accepts electrons acts as an oxidizing agent (oxidizing agent).
